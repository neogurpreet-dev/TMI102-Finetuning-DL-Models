The Journey

Ten weeks, from a small CNN to a LoRA-adapted ResNet-50.

WEEK 01

A custom CNN on CIFAR-10

Started with CIFAR-10 as a stand-in dataset and built a CNN from scratch.

WEEK 02

First pretrained ResNet-50, on galaxy images

Moved to a pretrained ResNet-50 (torchvision) and ran it on galaxy images.

WEEK 03

Building the galaxy dataset

Built the dataset from Galaxy Zoo. The first version had 2 classes, a choice I made myself.

WEEKS 04 · 05 · 06

Frozen, full fine-tuning, LoRA

Switched from PyTorch to the notebooks' TensorFlow/Keras. Trained a frozen ResNet-50 baseline, then full fine-tuning, then LoRA (rank 4) on the 256-unit head layer.

Fixed a rescaling issue along the way: I replaced the hardcoded 1./255 with ResNet-50's preprocess_input and re-ran Weeks 4 and 5.

THE PIVOT · 18 OCT

From 2 classes to all 3

The assignment notebooks assumed 3 classes (Spiral, Elliptical, Merger), but my dataset had 2. I first handled that by changing the output layer to Dense(2).

After asking my mentors, I redid it properly with all 3 classes: rebuilt the dataset, retrained the Week 4, 5 and 6 models, and redid Weeks 8 and 9.

Elliptical and spiral stayed exactly as before (8,132 images each, split 5,692 / 1,220 / 1,220), and merger was added as a new class. Mergers are about 3× rarer, so the loss uses class weights, which makes a mistake on the rare class cost more.

WEEK 07

Grad-CAM: checking what the model looks at

Looked at heatmaps for 6 random images and 2 wrong predictions, to see if the model looks at the galaxy or at shortcuts.

WEEK 08

Robustness tests

Tested the retrained 3-class Week 5 model under noise, blur and rotation.

WEEK 09

Confusion matrix and ROC

A 3×3 confusion matrix and one-vs-rest ROC curves, comparing all three retrained models.

In My Words

Taken as written from my assignment notebooks.

Why a lower learning rate for fine-tuning?

If we train from scratch, then it starts from random weights hence there's nothing to protect in them which can be ruined. But, during fine-tuning from ImageNet's pretrained weights, if we don't keep a lower LR (learning rate), then there is a high chance that a sudden abrupt change might ruin the well trained weights in just one step. The lower LR makes sure that the weights are allowed only to change by a small amount.

Week 5

LoRA, explained with a song

It's like creating a slowed and reverb version of a song where the original song remains untouched, just like we freeze pretrained weights in LoRA and don't touch them. You don't need to recreate the complete song again, you apply a few settings on the original song to change the entire vibe and in LoRA, you add adapter matrices on top of the original weights, which gives our model the ability to detect galaxies, that it didn't have initially.

Week 6 (excerpt)

Why Grad-CAM, if accuracy is already high?

Looking at the high accuracy alone, we cannot confidently say that the model is working properly. It is possible that the model might be using some shortcuts, like looking at the background or some bright stars around the galaxy or other minor details, instead of the galaxy itself. Looking at the heat map, we can check that the model focuses on the galaxy rather than some other shortcuts, and this helps us to debug the model properly whenever needed.

Week 7

Key Finding

Full fine-tuning is the best model at 93.17% accuracy. LoRA trains only 9,987 parameters (99.96% fewer than full fine-tuning) but reaches 82.14%. The gap comes mostly from the rare merger class: recall is 37.4% for LoRA against 81.0% for full fine-tuning.
